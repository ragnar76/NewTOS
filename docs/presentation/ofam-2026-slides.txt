# NewTOS - Why and How
- It started more than 20 years ago - the day I registered newtosworld.de
- The dream back then: TOS on an ordinary PC.
  - No Raspberry Pi, no ARM desktops - just a beige box and an idea
- It never got built. I couldn't program, and I never found anyone to do it with me
- This year that finally changed: I built it together with Claude, an AI
- What came out is not an emulator - it is TOS itself, rewritten for today's hardware
  - Same GEMDOS, BIOS, XBIOS, VDI and AES, checked line by line against EmuTOS
- Atari programs are recompiled from their original sources, without changes
- Since May: 1,250 commits, 190,000 lines of code, more than 1,250 tests

# What it runs on
- A Raspberry Pi 4 - four ARM cores at 1.5 GHz, for less than an Atari cost back then
- Full HD over HDMI, in true colour
- The SD card is the hard disk: about 10 MB per second, with a 32 MB cache in RAM
- An ordinary USB keyboard and mouse
- Every program gets its own memory protection - a crash ends the program, not the system
- Most of the work happened in QEMU on the Mac, then went onto the real Pi

# BIOS and XBIOS
- The layer underneath, just as programs expect it from a Falcon with TOS 4.04
- The SD card shows up as drives C: to F:
- System variables and cookie jar live where Atari software looks for them
- German or US keyboard layout - or your own KEYBOARD.KBD
- A 200 Hz timer, like the real machine
- A few new calls of our own: CPU clock, temperature, system information

# VDI, AES and Desktop
- The VDI draws everything - about 70 functions, in true colour
- Compared pixel by pixel with a real TT and a Falcon
- The AES is complete: windows, menus, dialogs, events, resources
- The 3D look you know from TOS 4
- Desk accessories work - System Info is one, built just for this
- The desktop is EmuDESK, rebuilt piece by piece - and TeraDesk runs on top

# Toolchain
- Programs are built with clang and our own SDK
- The Atari libraries - mintlib, gemlib, cflib - were ported, not rewritten
- Native programs get their own names: .NAP, .NCP, .NAC
  - .PRG, .TOS and .ACC stay free for a 68000 emulator later
- Already running: QED, TeraDesk, zView with 10 image formats, Doom, CoreMark
- Even LDG libraries load, natively, as .NLD files
- CoreMark says about 7,300 - fast enough for anything an Atari ever did

# Plans and Outlook
- A 68000 emulator, so your old .PRG files run too

- Networking
- Faster graphics: the VDI drawing code in hand-written ARM assembly
- More software: Thing, HighWire, the newer zView
- Follow along at newtosworld.de
